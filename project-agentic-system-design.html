Executive Summary

From tribal-knowledge engineering to a governed decision system

The client's front-line engineering process was the single largest constraint on revenue growth: every new proposal depended on the availability and judgment of a small number of senior engineers. I led the architecture, build, and change-management program for an agentic decision platform that codified that judgment into a governed, explainable system — turning a capacity-constrained bottleneck into a scalable, auditable capability.

The Business Problem

The client's growth strategy depended on winning more industrial water treatment contracts, but the engineering organization could not scale with demand. Requirement intake from sales and clients arrived fragmented across emails, calls, and spreadsheets. Preliminary system design — sizing, treatment train selection, and compliance checks — was concentrated among four senior engineers whose reasoning existed only in their heads, creating a single point of failure for a business generating recurring seven-figure contract value.

Independent process audit at engagement kickoff quantified the exposure: average design turnaround of 9.4 business days per proposal, a rework rate of 41% driven by incomplete or misread requirements, and design output that varied materially depending on which engineer handled the account. Leadership had no standardized way to review assumptions, trace design rationale, or onboard new engineers without a multi-month apprenticeship — a structural risk to both margin and growth targets.

The Solution

I designed and delivered an agentic design-decision platform that formalizes senior-engineer reasoning into a governed workflow rather than a static tool. The system conducts structured requirement intake (influent characteristics, discharge targets, footprint, and site constraints), applies domain-constraint checks drawn from the client's engineering standards, and generates a reviewable "design handoff pack" — assumptions, rationale, trade-offs, and a recommended treatment configuration — that a licensed engineer signs off before it enters detailed design.

The platform was deliberately built as a decision-support layer, not an autonomous designer: every output is traceable to its inputs and constraints, nothing is finalized without human sign-off, and the system declines to proceed rather than guess when information is missing. This governance posture was the single most important factor in securing engineering and compliance buy-in.

System Architecture — Request-to-Handoff Flow

Five stages · one mandatory human checkpoint

01 · Input Layer Requirement Intake

Sales notes, client specs, influent data, site & footprint constraints

02 · Reasoning Layer Agent Orchestration

Guided intake conversation, structured state manager, query-able record capture

03 · Governance Layer Domain-Constraint Engine

Engineering standards, discharge norms, site-feasibility rules, no-guess enforcement

04 · Human Gate Licensed Engineer Sign-off

Mandatory review and approval before any output leaves the platform

05 · Output Layer Design Handoff Pack

Assumptions, rationale, trade-offs, and a board-reviewable delivery artifact

Traceability & audit log runs beneath every stage — each decision, assumption, and approval is recorded and reviewable end to end.

Automated agent processing
Mandatory human checkpoint
  • Stateful conversation architecture that captures requirements, assumptions, constraints, and approvals as structured, queryable records — not free-text chat logs.
  • Domain-constraint engine encoding the client's engineering standards, discharge norms, and site-feasibility rules, enforced before any recommendation is surfaced.
  • Template-driven documentation layer that converts every agent decision into a standardized, board-reviewable delivery artifact.
  • Governance controls: scoped prompting, approved-output templates, full decision traceability, and a strict "no-guess" policy when required inputs are missing.

My Role

I owned this engagement end-to-end as the client's AI/ML leader, operating at CTO scope: I built the business case and ROI model presented to the executive committee, aligned engineering and compliance stakeholders on success metrics (cycle time, rework rate, adoption), and set the non-negotiable governance requirements — traceability, human sign-off, and constraint enforcement — that made the system safe to deploy in a regulated, safety-critical domain.

Beyond the technical build, I led the change-management program that got senior engineers to trust and adopt the platform: structured onboarding, a phased regional rollout, and a feedback loop that fed real engineering exceptions back into the constraint model. The result was not just a working system, but an operating model the client's leadership could scale, govern, and defend to its own board.
