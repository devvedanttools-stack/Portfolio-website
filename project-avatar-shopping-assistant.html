Executive Summary

From generic chat widget to a governed, guided-selling operating model

The client's highest-margin products required the kind of guided, consultative selling that in-store staff provided naturally, but that experience simply did not exist online. I led the architecture, build, and governance program for an avatar-based assistant that replaced a generic, inconsistent chat widget with brand-safe guided discovery and explainable recommendations — bringing premium, high-touch selling to every visitor, at every hour, without adding always-on staff.

The Business Problem

The client's highest-value SKUs were high-consideration purchases that customers historically relied on in-store staff to evaluate — comparing options, resolving objections, and building confidence before committing. Online, that guidance was reduced to a generic chat widget that gave inconsistent answers, could not explain its recommendations, and often escalated to a human agent who wasn't always available, creating friction at exactly the moment a customer needed reassurance.

A baseline audit at engagement kickoff quantified the gap: cart abandonment on high-consideration SKUs ran at 71%, well above the client's templated-product baseline, average time-to-decision exceeded nine minutes with no guided path to a recommendation, and post-purchase support tickets citing "wrong product for my needs" were a top-five contact driver. Leadership needed a way to replicate consultative, trustworthy guidance online at scale, without the brand risk of an assistant that could go off-message or make unexplainable recommendations.

The Solution

I designed and delivered an avatar-based shopping assistant that asks lightweight, guided questions to capture intent and preferences, then produces explainable product recommendations with clear reasons and side-by-side comparisons. The assistant handles objections and comparison questions within a governed, policy-safe response set, and is wrapped in a premium interface built to match the brand's in-store experience rather than a generic support widget.

Every recommendation is explainable and traceable back to the customer's stated preferences, and every response the assistant can give is bounded by brand and policy guardrails defined jointly with merchandising and brand leadership. That governance — not just the avatar interface — was what earned trust from a brand team that had previously resisted AI-driven customer-facing experiences.
