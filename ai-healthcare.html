Case Study 1

AI-Based Continuous Patient Deterioration Monitoring

A wearable + edge + EHR-integrated AI stack that surfaces deterioration risk trajectories hours earlier than intermittent observation, while actively managing alert fatigue.

Clinical context

A multi-hospital health system struggled with late recognition of deterioration on wards despite standard early warning scores. Subtle trends existed hours before escalation, but were hard to spot from periodic observations. The monitoring setup was fragmented and clinicians were already overwhelmed by alarms.

Solution overview

The system used medical-grade wearables streaming high-frequency vitals to an on-prem edge gateway, then to an AI microservice that fused streaming vitals with EHR context (e.g., age, comorbidities, labs). Instead of binary alarms, the UI emphasized rolling risk trajectories and time-to-high-risk indicators embedded directly in the EHR.

Architecture

Wearables → edge gateway (validation + batching) → AI microservice (temporal model + EHR context) → write-back as FHIR observations.

Design Principle

Trajectory-first visuals to support clinician judgment, not automation. Focus on augmentation and trust.

Deployment

Retrospective validation → shadow mode → limited pilot → staged rollout with continuous monitoring of error modes and alert volume.

Clinical impact (reported)

  • Detected a large fraction of critical events earlier than traditional approaches, creating an 8–24 hour proactive intervention window in many cases.
  • Enabled ward-level prioritization and escalation playbooks tied to risk trajectories.
  • Reduced uncontrolled decompensations by moving action earlier in the workflow.

Technical highlights

  • High-frequency ingestion and cleaning at the edge, secured transport, and reliable back-pressure handling.
  • Temporal deep learning on vital-sign streams, fused with slow-changing EHR features.
  • MLOps discipline: threshold tuning, drift monitoring, and staged rollouts.

Case Study 2

AI Chest X‑Ray Triage in Resource‑Limited Hospitals

An offline-capable, on‑prem imaging AI pipeline that processes new DICOM studies, produces findings and heatmaps, and pushes overlays back into PACS viewers for triage.

Clinical context

Radiology services were overloaded, with long turnaround times for routine exams. Connectivity constraints made cloud-only AI unreliable. Clinicians needed decision support for pneumonia/TB/heart failure without waiting days for reports.

Solution overview

A CheXNet-style CNN fine-tuned on local data was deployed on-prem next to the PACS server. Every new study was automatically scored and suspicious cases were tagged. Heatmaps were written back as secondary capture images so radiologists/clinicians could toggle overlays inside their existing viewer.

Pipeline

DICOM listener → containerized inference service → result writer (heatmaps + findings) → PACS/RIS + viewer integration.

Resilience

Edge-first design that functions in offline/low-bandwidth environments with predictable latency.

Governance

Assistive triage; humans remain accountable. Policies defined for disagreements and explicit limitations documented.

Measured outcomes (reported)

  • Operational availability and latency treated as key success metrics; results returned within seconds after storage in PACS.
  • Enabled fast-tracking suspicious exams and second-look review when risk scores were unexpectedly high.

Technical highlights

  • End-to-end imaging AI: DICOM ingestion → inference → overlays in clinical viewers.
  • CPU-optimized inference path for constrained environments.
  • Feedback loop capturing disagreements for periodic fine-tuning on local cases.

Case Study 3

AI‑Driven Sepsis Early Detection and ED Workflow

A hybrid approach combining transparent rule logic with workflow automation, optionally paired with ML predictors, integrated into the EHR for reliable adoption.

Clinical context

Sepsis early signs are subtle and EDs are overloaded. Traditional rules (SIRS/qSOFA) can be inconsistently applied. Separately deployed “risk dashboards” often fail because they don’t change the workflow where decisions happen.

Solution overview

A decision-support engine monitored triage vitals, labs, and documentation. When criteria suggested severe sepsis/shock, it triggered structured workflows: standardized order sets, timers, and checklists aligned with bundles. In some deployments, ML risk models ran alongside the rules to forecast risk earlier while keeping interpretability.

Two layers

Rule layer (transparent clinical logic) + workflow layer (orders, routing, timers, bundle completion tracking).

Deployment

Shadow mode first, then phased rollout with alert governance to avoid “cry wolf”.

Observability

Alert volume, bundle compliance, and outcomes tracked as first-class operational metrics.

Measured outcomes (reported)

  • Improved bundle compliance and reduced length of stay; sepsis mortality fell substantially when integrated workflows were adopted.
  • Key lesson: socio-technical integration mattered as much as model performance.

Technical highlights

  • Streaming feature extraction from EHR feeds and near-real-time risk state updates.
  • Hybrid system design combining deterministic rules with ML risk scoring where appropriate.
  • Operational feedback loops via QI metrics and periodic threshold adjustments.
