Executive Summary

From fragmented documents to a governed chemical intelligence system

The client's R&D and procurement teams were making high-stakes formulation and sourcing decisions based on chemical data scattered across thousands of safety data sheets, supplier catalogs, and internal notes. I led the architecture, build, and governance program for an AI intelligence platform that turned that fragmented archive into a single, searchable, audit-ready source of truth — cutting research time, standardizing hazard screening, and giving leadership traceability it never had before.

The Business Problem

The client's formulation and sourcing decisions depended on chemical property, hazard, and compliance data locked inside thousands of unstructured SDS PDFs, supplier catalogs, and scientist notebooks. Every substitute-material evaluation or supplier qualification required a researcher to manually search, extract, and cross-check data by hand, with no consistent standard for how hazard and compliance checks were applied across teams.

A baseline audit at engagement kickoff quantified the exposure: researchers spent an average of 5.8 hours per formulation decision locating and reconciling chemical data, supplier qualification cycles ran 3–4 weeks longer than the client's target, and inconsistent hazard screening had produced two near-miss compliance findings in the prior 18 months. Leadership had no auditable trail connecting a sourcing or formulation decision back to the underlying safety and compliance data — a material risk in a regulated, safety-critical industry.

The Solution

I designed and delivered a chemical intelligence platform that ingests heterogeneous sources — SDS documents, supplier catalogs, and internal R&D notes — and extracts them into a consistent, structured schema. On top of that schema, the platform provides unified search and side-by-side comparison across chemical properties, hazards, and procurement constraints, with governance-aware views built specifically for R&D workflows and procurement sourcing rules.

Every comparison and substitute-material evaluation carries full traceability back to its source document, and every hazard or compliance flag is surfaced consistently regardless of which analyst runs the query. That audit trail — not just faster search — was the requirement that got EHS and compliance leadership to champion the platform rather than resist it.
